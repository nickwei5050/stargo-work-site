Start with one workflow: five steps to put AI to work

August 27, 2026 · The STARGO WORK team · View all articles

The safest way to bring AI into a company is to pick the one workflow that matters most, run it through five steps and prove it with real cases — then expand to other departments.

Key takeaways

  • Start with the workflow that matters most, not every department at once.
  • Five steps: choose a workflow, prepare context, connect accounts, assign roles and approvals, validate results.
  • Connect your existing CRM, ERP, email and drives first; you don’t have to replace them all.
  • Authorized people approve key actions; approval, sending and checking results stay separate.

Why not change every department at once?

Hand acquisition, customer service, quoting, orders and content to a new system at the same time, and every team spends its energy adapting while no single process runs end to end. It also becomes hard to tell where time was actually saved and where the same work simply moved somewhere else.

One workflow keeps the scope small, the ownership clear and the results easy to check. Once it works, the prepared information, approval rules and working methods carry straight over to the next workflow.

Which workflow should come first?

Usually the one that costs the most time, limits growth the most or has the messiest handoffs. Common starting points for manufacturers and exporters:

  • Customer acquisition: the company relies on marketplace inquiries and has no target account list or outreach priorities of its own.
  • Inquiries and follow-up: many inquiries, slow replies and nobody following up once a quote has gone out.
  • Quotes and PI: prices live in spreadsheets and verbal approvals; versions and margin limits are unclear.
  • Export documents: commercial invoices, packing lists, certificates of origin and Form E materials are checked line by line by hand.
  • Marketing assets: product images, detail pages and multilingual copy never keep up with new launches.

What happens in each of the five steps?

1. Choose a workflow. Write down the problem, the people involved and what success looks like — for example, faster replies or quotes that can be traced back to approved prices.

2. Prepare the context. Bring in only the products, customers, knowledge and business rules this workflow needs. Uploaded material is reviewed before it counts as approved company information.

3. Connect your accounts. Connect existing email, drives, CRM and ERP with your authorization. You don’t have to replace everything; decide system by system what to keep, connect or adjust.

4. Assign roles and approvals. Decide which AI employees do the work, who approves, who takes over when something goes wrong, what budget they have and which actions they may take.

5. Validate the result. Check real cases. A page that opens, or an AI message saying “done”, is not the test; the business result is. Expand only once it holds.

What do people do in the workflow?

Set up approvals from the first workflow; don’t wait until you scale. AI organizes information, drafts content and recommends next steps; authorized people decide on quotes, outreach, important commitments and payments. Approval, sending and checking the outcome are separate steps, each with a record. That is the STARGO WORK principle: delegate the work, keep the authority.

How do you expand after the first workflow?

Once the first workflow proves useful, apply the same approach to more business areas and add more AI employees. With your authorization, Growth OS connects live data and outreach; Sales Desk connects channel messaging and business handoffs; ERP, fulfillment and finance run on the systems you have connected.

Frequently asked questions

Where should an enterprise start?

Choose one priority workflow. Prepare product, customer, knowledge and policy context, connect authorized accounts, assign roles and approvals, then validate real cases. Expand after that first workflow is proven useful.

Must we replace our current CRM, ERP, email and drives?

A complete replacement should not be assumed. STARGO WORK aims to connect existing accounts and information in one workspace. Which systems are retained, integrated or adjusted depends on the enterprise’s software and access permissions.

What does the first workflow need?

The products, customers, knowledge and business rules that workflow touches, plus the business accounts the company authorizes. It also needs clear roles: which AI employees do the work, who approves and who takes over when something goes wrong.

How does the scope grow after that?

Once the first workflow proves itself on real cases, add more business areas and AI roles step by step and validate each one the same way.
